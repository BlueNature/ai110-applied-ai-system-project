One common mistake players make in these games is falling for the gambler’s fallacy. The gambler’s fallacy is the belief that past random events influence future random outcomes. A classic example is thinking that if a coin has landed on heads several times in a row, tails is now “due.” In reality, if the coin flips are independent, the chance of the next flip being heads or tails remains the same, no matter what happened before.

In a number guessing game, gambler’s fallacy can appear when players assume a hidden pattern exists even when the clues do not support it. For example, if several guesses are lower than the target number, a player might think, “The next number I choose should probably be lower because I have been too high too often,” or they might believe the target is “more likely” to be a certain value because previous guesses have not worked. However, the correct approach is to use the information provided by the high/low clues, not to balance out past guesses. The target number does not change based on what guesses have already been made.

This same mistake appears in many areas of life. Gamblers may believe they are more likely to win after a losing streak, investors may assume a stock must rise because it has fallen repeatedly, or people may expect a rare event to happen simply because it has not happened recently. These beliefs can lead to poor decisions because they confuse random variation with meaningful patterns.

Understanding the gambler’s fallacy helps people make better choices by separating evidence from intuition.
